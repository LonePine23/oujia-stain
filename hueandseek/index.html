How it works

Find the colour in the photo.

A daily colour puzzle.

Playing

  • Tap anywhere to drop a marker. Drag to move it.
  • Arrow keys nudge it a pixel. Hold Shift for ten.
  • Lock in when you're happy. There's no clock.
  • Five colours, then you're done for the day. Your progress is saved, so you can close the tab and come back.

The two modes

  • Easy — you get the swatch, its name and its hex. The loupe splits down the middle: the colour you're after on one side, the pixel under your marker on the other. When the seam vanishes, you've got it.
  • Standard — you get the name. That's the lot. The loupe magnifies the photo and wears your colour on its rim, so you can see what you've picked but not what you're chasing. The swatch turns up when you lock in.

Scoring

Your colour and the target are compared with CIEDE2000 — a measure tuned to how an eye reads a gap rather than how far apart two numbers are.

The two modes are marked differently, because they are not the same task. In Easy the swatch is in front of you and the loupe butts your pixel against it, so it is a matching job and the marking is strict. In Standard there is nothing on screen to match — you are recalling what a name looks like — so being in the right family counts for a good deal more.

The archive

Every photograph that's had its day goes into the archive. Play one at random with five fresh colours — a new set each time, so a picture you've already seen is still a game. Archive rounds don't touch your record.
